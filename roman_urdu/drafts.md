### The two failures, and why neither is fundamental

**Placeholders collapse to ⟦0⟧ — a bug in the training data, now fixed.**
`add_placeholder` masked a single word per row, so all 3,450 placeholder
examples contained exactly `⟦0⟧` and the model never learned they are
numbered. Given "A string ⟦0⟧ and an ⟦1⟧ object ⟦2⟧" it produced "Aik string⟦0⟧
aur aik⟦0⟧ object": right shape, every index zero. The generator now masks up
to three per row, giving 1,695 / 756 / 999 rows with one, two and three
placeholders.

**Long sentences are copied rather than translated — 14 of 50.** ERUPD averages
15.8 words and half these sources exceed 18, so the model falls back to copying
when it runs past what it was trained on. Fixable by splitting long sentences
before translation, which is what the frame layer already does structurally.

### What this means

The fortnight is viable. Correcting these is clearly faster than composing
from scratch, which is the premise the whole plan rested on, and the premise
opus-mt failed.

Retraining on the fixed data costs another hour and should move placeholder
retention well above 21/43 before any hand-correction starts — worth doing
first, since every corrected row inherits whatever the model gets wrong.
